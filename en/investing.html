This section documents my U.S. equity research, actual positions, and trade reviews.

After several years of doing this, I have come to believe one thing: stock selection determines how much you can make; position sizing determines whether you survive. The writing here therefore has two halves—what to own and how to trade it.

1. Investment Research

My main areas of interest are:

  • Semiconductors — memory, logic, and equipment
  • AI infrastructure — data centers, compute, and storage
  • High-growth technology companies — changes in business models and valuation frameworks

Research posts focus on company fundamentals, industry cycles, and valuation logic. I care less about a single price target than about which framework the market is using to value a company, and when that framework may change.

2. Trading System

Most losses do not come from getting the direction wrong. They come from position sizing, execution, or emotional control. This section records the method, not merely the ticker:

  • Position sizing — separating core and event-driven positions and setting single-name limits
  • Execution — scaling in, scaling out, and why trying to sell the exact top is a mistake
  • Trend assessment — distinguishing a real breakdown from market noise
  • Trading discipline — writing rules and preventing exceptions in the heat of the moment

I try to explain where every rule came from—the specific trade or mistake that produced it. Trading slogans detached from context are not very useful.

I am especially willing to review trades where the thesis was right but the execution was wrong. Prices expire; the reasons behind a decision do not.
